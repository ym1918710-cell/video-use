# Delivery and framing memory

Choose delivery settings from the destination and source—not from a habit. Confirm the platform requirement when it materially affects the story or framing.

| Destination | Master canvas | Typical frame rate | Editorial note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | 1080×1920, 9:16 | Match source; commonly 30 | Keep captions and vital UI clear of the lower app interface and side controls. Review on a vertical preview. |
| YouTube standard | 1920×1080, 16:9 | Match source; commonly 24 or 30 | Favor readability on TV and desktop; avoid mobile-only caption sizing. |
| YouTube Short | 1080×1920, 9:16 | Match source | Same framing care as Reels; establish hook immediately. |
| Square feed | 1080×1080, 1:1 | Match source | Reframe intentionally; do not simply center-crop a talking head. |
| Presentation / course | 1920×1080, 16:9 | Match source | Let diagrams and text remain on screen long enough to read. |

## Technical defaults

- Preserve the source frame rate unless delivery demands a different standard. Do not casually convert 24↔30 fps or variable frame-rate footage.
- Deliver H.264 / yuv420p / AAC when a broadly compatible MP4 is needed. Use a higher-quality master only when the user asks for a finishing or archive format.
- Tone-map HLG/PQ footage to Rec.709 SDR unless HDR delivery is explicitly requested and the full chain supports it.
- Avoid upscaling as a cosmetic “quality” fix. Upscale only when required for a documented output specification.

## Safe framing

- Vertical social: leave practical clearance at bottom for captions, username, description, and music UI; keep meaningful faces/text away from the right-side control rail.
- Landscape: preserve lower-thirds and edge detail; do not let subtitles sit on essential charts, products, or faces.
- Every platform rule is a starting point. The rendered preview—not the source frame—decides whether placement is safe.
