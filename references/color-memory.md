# Color and finishing memory

Color decisions start with correction, then creative intent. Make one change, inspect a representative face/product/highlight, then continue.

## Order of operations

1. Normalize orientation, exposure, white balance, and HDR-to-SDR conversion.
2. Match adjacent takes before creating a look.
3. Apply the creative look softly.
4. Recheck skin tones, neutral objects, brand colors, text, and highlight detail.

## Practical looks

| Look | Suitable material | Direction | Watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral clean | Education, corporate, product | Small exposure/contrast correction; faithful skin | Over-sharpening and over-saturation |
| Warm editorial | Lifestyle, founder story, café/interior | Slight warm mids; controlled contrast | Orange skin and crushed shadows |
| Cool technical | Software, industrial, night work | Neutral whites; restrained cool shadows | Cyan skin or illegible UI |
| Filmic restrained | Brand story, travel, documentary | Softer highlight roll-off; moderate saturation | Heavy teal/orange cliché, lost blacks |
| High-energy social | Fashion, sport, music | Intentional contrast and color separation | Clipped channels, inconsistent takes |

## Matching and QC

- Match exposure and white balance across cuts before judging a grade. A “style” cannot repair a mismatch.
- Protect skin, product colors, white UI surfaces, and subtitles. They are reference points for whether the look remains believable.
- Check waveform/parade equivalents when available; otherwise inspect representative frames from every camera and lighting condition.
- Do not use a global grade to conceal a bad source clip. Flag it for a different take, B-roll cover, or explicit user decision.
- HDR sources require deliberate tone mapping; metadata alone does not produce a social-ready SDR result.
